# Low-cost microtome fixture for real trials

A bench fixture that replaces the costly rotary microtome in a real trial. It is built from the task's own modelled parts, at full size and in the same positions: a plywood housing; a printed handwheel whose handle pin locks only at 12 o'clock; a printed specimen head on two 8 mm rods, driven through its 59 mm stroke by a crank pin in a yoke slot; a sprung cassette clamp with its lever (longer and lower than the instrument's, as in the simulation, so a gripper fits behind it); and a knife holder with a dull mock blade and a hinged guard with stops. The robot meets the same shapes, positions, strokes and operating forces as in the simulation. Only the housing's outside differs from a real instrument, and the robot never handles it. A real Leica RM2125 RTS can be used instead where one is available: the scene follows it.

Generated by `tools/export_fixture.py` from the task's own modelled parts (`task.py`) and `fabrication.json`; re-run it after any change to either:

```sh
python3 tools/export_fixture.py submissions/microtome-block-out
```

Safety: the fixture has no cutting edge. The mock blade is dull shim stock, so a gripper that reaches between clamp and knife is still scored 0 for good, as in the simulation, but is not hurt.

Housing outside: the plywood walls stand where the instrument's covers are, so the robot's clearances are the same; the robot never handles the housing.

Prices were read from the sellers' pages or search listings on 2026-11-18, before shipping and tax; they move, so treat the total as an estimate. For comparison, a used Leica RM2125 lists at about 2,100 USD and a refurbished one at about 3,400 USD (search listings, 2026-11-18).

Tools assumed: an FDM printer with a 220 x 220 mm bed (PLA, 3 perimeters, 15 to 20 % infill), a saw for the plywood, a drill with 4.2, 5.2, 8, 13 and 16 mm bits, an M4 tap and a file.

Positions are the task's assembly coordinates: millimetres from the midpoint between the arm bases, x forward, y left, z up from the bench top; the instrument stands 80 mm further out than the instrument's own layout numbers in task.py (task.XS).
