# Take a passport back out of a ticket window's deal tray

A section of counter stands on the table with a recessed stainless deal tray set flush in it and the window's glass standing across the tray's middle.
A passport has been passed back through the tray and lies in the dish.
The robot is told only the end state: the passport lying on the green mat on the customer's side of the counter.

Anyone who has bought a ticket, banked at a counter, collected a prescription at a night hatch or shown a passport at a window has done this.
The tray is Total Security Solutions' Recessed Currency Tray in its stock size, 16 x 10 x 1-1/2 in; the maker's data sheet says it "works great for passing paperwork, passports, currency, and credit cards".
Its dish is one circular arc across the window, so anything let go in it comes to rest on the dish's lowest line, which is directly under the glass.
There the passport cannot be picked up:

- along the window it is 125 mm, wider than the gripper opens (94 mm);
- across the window it is 88 mm, which the gripper could span, but half of it is behind the glass, and the glass stops a hand coming straight down 2 cm short of it;
- its two long edges lie on the steel, with no gap under them for a fingertip.

It has to be drawn toward the customer with fingertips pressed on the 41 mm strip that shows, up the dish's curved ramp and over the rim.
Let go high on the ramp it slides back down and stops low in the dish, behind the rim.
What is tested is that non-prehensile draw: enough press for the fingertips to carry the passport up a 36 degree ramp against the steel's friction and its own weight, kept until all of it is over the rim.
One hand can do the whole task.

## Files

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `task.py` | The yam_arena task file: objects, `success()` and `score()` |
| `demo.py` | The scripted full-score demo (open loop on the layout and the clock) and its negative variants |
| `partkit.py` | Rigid parts built from boxes, for yam_arena task files (the passport, the glass) |
| `meshkit.py` | Meshes built in code, for yam_arena task files (the tray's arc dish, the worktop with its cut-out) |

`partkit.py` and `meshkit.py` hook `yam_arena.scene` and the embodiment's step and reset without modifying yam_arena, so the task runs with the standard `yam_arena` embodiment and `task_score` scorer.
All geometry is generated by code in this package; no third-party assets are used.

## Run a trial

In the Isaac Lab-Arena environment from the simulation walkthrough (`source /data/IsaacLab-Arena/yam.env`), from this folder:

```bash
inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_arena --policy dream \
  -E task=task.py -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 -E cam_width=320 -E cam_height=176 \
  --instruction "Take the passport out of the tray under the window and leave it lying on the green mat." \
  --scorer task_score --max-steps 1200 --store-frames --log-dir ~/yam-eval/deal-tray-dream
```

`task_score` prints the trial's score: 1.0 on success, otherwise `score()` at the end of the episode.

## Scripted demo

```bash
python demo.py task.py demo_out                                    # full score
DEMO_VARIANT=pinch     python demo.py task.py out --no-record      # an open hand closes on it where it lies: 0
DEMO_VARIANT=let_go    python demo.py task.py out --no-record      # drawn up the ramp to the rim and let go: slides back, 0
DEMO_VARIANT=drop_pinch python demo.py task.py out --no-record     # the same, then a hand closes on it where it stopped
DEMO_VARIANT=push_away python demo.py task.py out --no-record      # pushed toward the glass: slides back, 0
DEMO_VARIANT=overdraw  python demo.py task.py out --no-record      # drawn on over the counter's front edge: 0 for good
DEAL_TRAY_BOOKLET=heavy python demo.py task.py out --no-record     # rehearsal only, not the task: a 5 mm, 45 g booklet
DEAL_TRAY_BOOKLET=light python demo.py task.py out --no-record     # rehearsal only, not the task: a 4 mm, 36 g booklet
python demo.py task.py --ik                                        # no simulator: IK along every path
```

1. The left hand closes its fingers and pitches 35 degrees forward, so that its body stays on the customer's side of the glass, and sets its fingertips on the strip of the passport that shows, 26 mm from the passport's middle, commanded 16 mm into it: the position-controlled arm is held up about 12 mm, a press of a few newtons.
2. Keeping the press, it draws the passport toward the customer at 25 mm/s along the arc of the maker's drawing: up the ramp (pressing 6 mm more by the rim, where the arm's lag eases the press), over the rim (where the passport bridges from the rim to the dish until the fingertips pass the rim and press it flat), then level across the flange and the worktop on to the mat.
3. It lifts off and returns to its ready pose.

The demo is open loop: every target comes from the fixed layout in `task.py` and the clock.
It reads no object pose and no joint state of anything; the trace and `result.json` read the simulator for logging only.

Results in Isaac Sim (one batch: the demo and the five variants):

- The demo reaches 1.0 (success) in 352 steps (18 s) of the 1,200-step limit: the passport ends flat on the mat, 4 mm from the mat's middle across the window and 11 mm along it, turned less than 1 degree.
  On the ramp it is tilted up to 23 degrees; the arm's tool point runs about 13 mm behind and 14 mm above its commanded path the whole way, which is the press.
- `pinch` scores 0: an open hand sent straight down over the strip that shows is stopped by the glass (the arm's wrist meets the pane); it ends 2 cm short of where it was sent, its fingertips 3 cm above the passport, and closes on nothing. The passport does not move.
- `let_go` scores 0: drawn 58 mm up the ramp to 18 degrees of tilt and let go, the passport slides back to 14 degrees, where the steel's friction holds it, and stays in the dish, 45 mm from the lowest line and behind the rim.
- `drop_pinch` scores 0: after the same let-go, an open hand pitched like the drawing hand (so that the glass does not stop it) sets both fingertips on the passport where it stopped, closes, lifts and goes to the mat. Along the window the passport is wider than the hand opens, and its edges lie on the steel: the fingertips close across its cover, pushing it 8 mm further down the ramp, and nothing is held.
- `push_away` scores 0: the passport is pushed 22 mm up the clerk's ramp, as far as the glass lets the fingertips go, and stays in the dish under the glass.
- `overdraw` scores 0 for good: the passport is drawn over the worktop's front edge and falls to the table.

How wide the working range is (earlier batches of the same package, one setting changed at a time):

- The booklet: with `DEAL_TRAY_BOOKLET=light` (4 mm, 36 g) and `DEAL_TRAY_BOOKLET=heavy` (5 mm, 45 g) the same demo scores 1.0 in 352 steps each, ending within 1 mm of where the 4.5 mm, 40 g booklet ends.
- The press (run with the 4 mm, 36 g booklet): the draw also scores 1.0 with the press commanded 12 or 22 mm instead of 16.
  At 9 mm the fingertips lose the passport on the ramp at 14 degrees of tilt and it stays in the dish (0); raising the pad friction to 1.5 does not help (lost at 9 degrees).
  A touch that is too light is the way this task fails.
- The pad-on-cover friction (same booklet): 1.0 with it at 0.8 instead of 1.0.

Dream-1, given only the instruction, scored 0 in one run of 1,200 steps: its left hand went to the tray and worked at the passport under the glass for the whole minute, and the passport stayed in the dish.

## Scoring

Judged at the end of the run.
Every level counts only once both grippers are at least 3 cm from the passport.

- 1.0: the passport lies flat (within 10 degrees) on the worktop with all of its outline over the green mat, and for 1 s (20 steps) both grippers have stayed at least 3 cm away and its whole pose has not moved 1 mm (all eight corners).
  The release is part of that second: a passport held still on the mat and then let go scores 0.75 until a full second after the hand has left.
- 0.75: the same, but not yet for 1 s.
- 0.5: at rest (released and still for 1 s) lying flat on the customer's side of the worktop with no part of it over the dish, but not wholly over the mat.
- 0.25: at rest with some of it at least 5 mm past the dish's rim on the customer's side, in any other state: part of it still over the dish, leaning on the rim, or overhanging the worktop's front edge.
- 0: in the dish (wherever it came to rest on the ramp), behind the glass, or still held.
- 0 for good: the passport has left the counter (its centre more than 10 mm below the worktop anywhere but in the tray). Picking it up again does not restore the score.

Nothing else is penalised: which arm is used, touching the glass or the tray, or lifting the passport.
Drawing it all the way to the mat, and drawing it over the rim, picking it up by the raised edge and laying it on the mat, score the same.
In a real trial every level is read from the final photograph with a ruler.

## Fidelity

Rigid parts; everything that happens to the passport is contact and friction (no grip, joint or force is scripted).

- Tray: the maker's drawing and data sheet give 16 x 10 x 1-1/2 in overall, 18 gauge (1.27 mm) stainless steel with a #4 brushed finish, a 1/2 in flange all round, a body 15 x 9 in under the flange and a 15-1/4 x 9-1/4 in cut-out, and show the dish as one arc across the 10 in direction between flat end walls.
  The drawing marks the arc "R" without a value: the 191.9 mm used here (36.1 degrees of slope at the rim, 36.8 mm deep) follows from the 9 in body and the 1-1/2 in height less the sheet. Estimate: measure the bought tray.
  The dish is one exact triangle mesh with 48 facets whose corners are on that radius; the tray is solid under its top surface.
- Passport: 125 x 88 mm (ICAO Doc 9303 part 4, the ID-3 size). No maker or state publishes a booklet's thickness or mass, so the 4.5 mm and 40 g used here are estimates: measure and weigh the booklet used.
  A published booklet build (32 pages of 90 g/m2 paper, 125 g/m2 endpapers, a polycarbonate data page of up to 0.76 mm, a board cover) works out at 3.9 mm and 36 g before thread, glue, laminate and the chip inlay, and real booklets are expected between 4 and 5 mm and between 36 and 45 g.
  `DEAL_TRAY_BOOKLET=light` and `DEAL_TRAY_BOOKLET=heavy` run the two ends of that range (rehearsal only; the task is the 4.5 mm, 40 g booklet).
  It is a rigid slab with square corners (the real corners have a 3 mm radius), with estimated thickness, mass and friction: the real-trial checks are the weighing and the tilt test under "Real-world build". A real booklet bends a millimetre or two over the 88 mm between its edges under the fingertips' press, and its binding carries the pages with the cover; neither changes the draw.
- Friction, all estimates: cover on brushed steel 0.275, cover on laminate 0.3, fingertip pad on cover 1.0.
  The simulator averages the two bodies' coefficients, so the passport's top millimetre carries a second material to give the pad-on-cover value while its edges and underside give the other two.
  At 0.275 a passport let go on the ramp holds only below 15.8 degrees of tilt (51 mm from the lowest line); with its edge at the rim it is tilted 22.8 degrees.
- Counter and glass: a 520 x 600 mm section of 1-1/2 in laminate worktop and the lower 300 mm of a 1/4 in tempered glass window, each cut down in size only; the glass's bottom edge is 2 mm above the worktop so that it clears the tray's flange. Every part that is touched is whole and at full size.
- The green mat is a 0.1 mm vinyl patch; in the simulator it is drawn on the worktop and does not collide.

## Runtime and seeds

One fixed layout (`object_jitter=0`), no random seed.
Run limit 1,200 steps (1 minute at 20 Hz).

## Real-world build

- Counter: an offcut of 1-1/2 in (38 mm) laminate worktop, 520 mm deep and 600 mm wide, with a 15-1/4 x 9-1/4 in cut-out centred 320 mm from its front edge and 60 mm left of its middle, clamped to the table with its front edge 100 mm in front of the arm bases.
- Tray: Total Security Solutions Recessed Currency Tray, 16 x 10 x 1-1/2 in, dropped into the cut-out with its 16 in side along the window.
  Check its dish with a depth gauge (36.8 mm at the middle) and a radius gauge; if the bought tray's arc differs from 191.9 mm, use the measured value in `task.py`.
- Glass: a 600 x 300 mm piece of 1/4 in tempered glass (or clear acrylic of the same thickness) standing across the tray's middle in 1 in aluminium U-channel screwed to the worktop at each side of the tray, its bottom edge 2 mm above the worktop.
- Mat: a 150 x 200 mm rectangle of green self-adhesive vinyl on the worktop, its far edge 18 mm in front of the tray's flange, centred on the tray.
- Passport: an expired passport or a printer's specimen booklet, 125 x 88 mm. Weigh it on a 0.1 g scale and measure its closed thickness with a caliper at the fore-edge under light pressure; record both with the trial. The task file assumes 4.5 mm and 40 g: if the booklet is outside 4 to 5 mm or 36 to 45 g, put the measured values in `task.py` (`BOOK_T`, `BOOK_MASS`) and rerun the demo before comparing scores.
- Friction check: lay the passport on a flat offcut of #4 brushed stainless sheet and tilt it; it should start to slide at about 15 degrees (0.27). Press a fingertip pad on its cover with a 2 N weight and pull with a spring scale: about 2 N (1.0).
- Reset: lay the passport in the dish with its long side along the glass and its spine on the clerk's side, centred on the tray; let go and let it settle on the dish's lowest line under the glass.
- Judge from the final photograph: where the passport lies against the mat's outline and the dish's rim, with both grippers at least 3 cm away.
